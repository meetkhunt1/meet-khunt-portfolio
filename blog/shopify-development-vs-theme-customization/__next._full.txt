41:T2782,<p>&quot;Can you customize our Shopify theme?&quot; and &quot;We need a Shopify developer&quot; sound like the same request. They&#39;re not, and mixing them up is one of the most common reasons Shopify projects get quoted wrong, run over, or end with a store that&#39;s hard to change.</p>
<p>This guide explains the difference between Shopify theme customization and Shopify development, what each can and can&#39;t do, and how to tell which one your store needs.</p>
<h2 id="the-short-answer">The Short Answer<a class="prose-anchor" href="#the-short-answer" aria-label="Link to this section: The Short Answer">#</a></h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Theme customization</strong> changes what your theme already offers: colours, fonts, layout settings, the sections it ships with and small code edits.</li>
<li><strong>Shopify development</strong> builds what your theme doesn&#39;t offer: new custom sections and templates, features driven by your product data, integrations and full store builds.</li>
</ul>
<p>If the change can be made in the theme editor, or with a few lines of CSS, it&#39;s customization. If it needs new Liquid sections, new templates or logic, it&#39;s development.</p>
<h2 id="what-is-shopify-theme-customization">What Is Shopify Theme Customization?<a class="prose-anchor" href="#what-is-shopify-theme-customization" aria-label="Link to this section: What Is Shopify Theme Customization?">#</a></h2>
<p>Every Shopify theme, from the free Dawn theme to paid themes in the Theme Store, comes with settings and sections. Customization works within those limits.</p>
<p>Typical theme customization includes:</p>
<ul>
<li>Changing colours, fonts, buttons and spacing in theme settings</li>
<li>Rearranging, adding or removing the sections the theme already includes</li>
<li>Editing announcement bars, menus, footers and homepage content</li>
<li>Small CSS changes to adjust how an existing element looks</li>
<li>Minor Liquid edits, such as showing an extra product detail in an existing block</li>
</ul>
<p>Much of this can be done by a store owner in the theme editor without touching code. When code is involved, the edits are small and stay close to what the theme was designed to do.</p>
<h2 id="what-is-shopify-development">What Is Shopify Development?<a class="prose-anchor" href="#what-is-shopify-development" aria-label="Link to this section: What Is Shopify Development?">#</a></h2>
<p>Shopify development is building new functionality and new design in the theme&#39;s code. A Shopify developer works in Liquid (Shopify&#39;s templating language), HTML, CSS and JavaScript, and often with metafields and the theme&#39;s JSON templates.</p>
<p>Typical Shopify development includes:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Custom sections and blocks</strong> that the theme doesn&#39;t have, built so they stay editable in the theme editor</li>
<li><strong>Custom product, collection and landing page templates</strong>, for example a long-form product page for paid traffic</li>
<li><strong>Turning a Figma design into a store</strong>, where the design doesn&#39;t match any existing theme</li>
<li><strong>Features driven by product data</strong>, such as size charts, ingredient lists or comparison tables filled from metafields</li>
<li><strong>Interactive elements</strong>, such as bundle builders, quizzes, sticky add-to-cart bars or custom variant pickers</li>
<li><strong>Full store builds and redesigns</strong>, where most templates are rebuilt</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="when-theme-customization-is-enough">When Theme Customization Is Enough<a class="prose-anchor" href="#when-theme-customization-is-enough" aria-label="Link to this section: When Theme Customization Is Enough">#</a></h2>
<p>Customization is the right choice when:</p>
<ul>
<li>Your theme already has the sections you need and you mainly want it to look like your brand</li>
<li>You&#39;re launching quickly and the store&#39;s job right now is to start selling</li>
<li>The change is visual: colours, fonts, images, copy and section order</li>
<li>Your team wants to make changes themselves in the theme editor</li>
</ul>
<p>A well-chosen theme with careful customization is a perfectly good store for many brands, especially early on.</p>
<h2 id="when-you-need-shopify-development">When You Need Shopify Development<a class="prose-anchor" href="#when-you-need-shopify-development" aria-label="Link to this section: When You Need Shopify Development">#</a></h2>
<p>You need development when:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Your design doesn&#39;t fit the theme.</strong> A Figma file from a designer almost never matches an off-the-shelf theme exactly. Making it match means building sections, not adjusting settings.</li>
<li><strong>Pages need to sell harder.</strong> Product pages and landing pages built for paid traffic usually need custom layouts: benefits, proof, comparison and offer sections in a specific order.</li>
<li><strong>You&#39;re stacking apps to fake features.</strong> If every new idea needs another app, a custom section is often faster, lighter and cheaper to run.</li>
<li><strong>Your team can&#39;t edit what was built.</strong> Hard-coded content that only a developer can change is a sign the work was done as a patch, not as proper sections.</li>
<li><strong>The store needs a redesign.</strong> A full redesign is development, even when it starts from an existing theme. If you&#39;re unsure whether you&#39;re there yet, see <a href="/blog/when-to-redesign-shopify-store/">when a DTC brand should redesign its Shopify store</a>.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="mistakes-that-make-customization-expensive">Mistakes That Make Customization Expensive<a class="prose-anchor" href="#mistakes-that-make-customization-expensive" aria-label="Link to this section: Mistakes That Make Customization Expensive">#</a></h2>
<p>Small edits can turn into costly problems when they&#39;re done carelessly:</p>
<aside class="prose-callout prose-callout--warning" role="note" aria-label="Warning"><p class="prose-callout__label">Warning</p><p>Editing a theme&#39;s code directly means that theme will no longer receive Shopify&#39;s automatic theme updates in the same way. Always duplicate the theme first, work on the copy and keep a clean backup.</p>
</aside>
<ul>
<li><strong>Editing the live theme.</strong> Work on a duplicate or unpublished theme, then publish when it&#39;s tested.</li>
<li><strong>Hard-coding content.</strong> Text, images and links written into the code can&#39;t be changed in the theme editor. Build them as section settings instead.</li>
<li><strong>Piling CSS overrides on top of each other.</strong> Dozens of small fixes on top of a theme become hard to untangle later.</li>
<li><strong>Choosing a theme that fights your design.</strong> If you&#39;re customizing against the theme at every step, development from a better starting point is usually cheaper.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="how-agencies-handle-this">How Agencies Handle This<a class="prose-anchor" href="#how-agencies-handle-this" aria-label="Link to this section: How Agencies Handle This">#</a></h2>
<p>Agencies run into this distinction on almost every client project. The design team produces a store design in Figma, and someone has to decide whether it can be done by customizing a theme or needs proper development.</p>
<p>Many agencies don&#39;t keep a Shopify developer on staff for every project, and outsource the development part instead. If that&#39;s your situation, here&#39;s how <a href="/white-label-shopify-development/">white label Shopify development for agencies</a> works.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-decide-for-your-store">How to Decide for Your Store<a class="prose-anchor" href="#how-to-decide-for-your-store" aria-label="Link to this section: How to Decide for Your Store">#</a></h2>
<p>Ask three questions:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Can the change be made in the theme editor?</strong> If yes, it&#39;s customization.</li>
<li><strong>Does the design or feature exist in the theme at all?</strong> If not, it&#39;s development.</li>
<li><strong>Will your team need to edit it later?</strong> If yes, make sure whoever builds it creates editable sections, not hard-coded pages.</li>
</ol>
<p>When you&#39;re unsure, describe the outcome you want rather than the change, for example &quot;a product page that explains the product to cold traffic&quot; rather than &quot;move the reviews up&quot;, and let a developer tell you which kind of work it needs.</p>
