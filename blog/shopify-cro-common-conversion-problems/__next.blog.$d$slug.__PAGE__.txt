32:T2cc3,<p>Most Shopify stores don&#39;t have one big conversion problem. They have several small ones, stacked along the path from ad to checkout, each losing a share of the visitors who were ready to buy.</p>
<p>This guide covers the conversion problems I see most often on Shopify stores, how to spot each one in your own store, and what to fix first.</p>
<h2 id="start-by-finding-where-visitors-drop-off">Start by Finding Where Visitors Drop Off<a class="prose-anchor" href="#start-by-finding-where-visitors-drop-off" aria-label="Link to this section: Start by Finding Where Visitors Drop Off">#</a></h2>
<p>Before fixing anything, find the step where the most people leave. Shopify Analytics shows a conversion funnel for your online store:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Sessions</strong>: people who visited the store</li>
<li><strong>Added to cart</strong>: sessions where something went into the cart</li>
<li><strong>Reached checkout</strong>: sessions that started checkout</li>
<li><strong>Completed checkout</strong>: sessions that placed an order</li>
</ol>
<p>The biggest drop tells you where to look first. A low add-to-cart rate points to product and collection pages. A big drop between cart and checkout points to cart friction. A drop inside checkout points to costs, trust or payment.</p>
<aside class="prose-callout prose-callout--tip" role="note" aria-label="Tip"><p class="prose-callout__label">Tip</p><p>Always split the funnel by device. Mobile and desktop often have completely different problems, and averaging them hides both.</p>
</aside>
<h2 id="product-page-problems">Product Page Problems<a class="prose-anchor" href="#product-page-problems" aria-label="Link to this section: Product Page Problems">#</a></h2>
<h3 id="1-the-page-doesn-t-explain-the-product-fast-enough">1. The page doesn&#39;t explain the product fast enough<a class="prose-anchor" href="#1-the-page-doesn-t-explain-the-product-fast-enough" aria-label="Link to this section: 1. The page doesn't explain the product fast enough">#</a></h3>
<p>Visitors from ads arrive cold. If the first screen on mobile is a large image and a product name, they don&#39;t learn what the product does, who it&#39;s for or why it&#39;s better before they decide to leave.</p>
<p><strong>Fix:</strong> put a clear one-line benefit, the price, the rating and the add-to-cart button within the first screen on mobile. Move secondary details below.</p>
<h3 id="2-product-images-don-t-answer-questions">2. Product images don&#39;t answer questions<a class="prose-anchor" href="#2-product-images-don-t-answer-questions" aria-label="Link to this section: 2. Product images don't answer questions">#</a></h3>
<p>Images that only show the product on a white background leave visitors guessing about size, texture, use and results.</p>
<p><strong>Fix:</strong> add images that show the product in use, at scale, with the details people ask about in reviews and customer service emails.</p>
<h3 id="3-reviews-and-proof-are-buried">3. Reviews and proof are buried<a class="prose-anchor" href="#3-reviews-and-proof-are-buried" aria-label="Link to this section: 3. Reviews and proof are buried">#</a></h3>
<p>Reviews are often loaded at the very bottom of the page, far below where most mobile visitors stop scrolling.</p>
<p><strong>Fix:</strong> show the star rating near the title, and put a few short, specific reviews close to the add-to-cart button.</p>
<h3 id="4-variant-selection-is-confusing">4. Variant selection is confusing<a class="prose-anchor" href="#4-variant-selection-is-confusing" aria-label="Link to this section: 4. Variant selection is confusing">#</a></h3>
<p>Small swatches, dropdowns with unclear names, or variants that silently change the price create hesitation and mistakes.</p>
<p><strong>Fix:</strong> use clear labels, show what changes with each option, and make sure the selected option and price are always obvious.</p>
<h3 id="5-shipping-returns-and-delivery-times-are-hidden">5. Shipping, returns and delivery times are hidden<a class="prose-anchor" href="#5-shipping-returns-and-delivery-times-are-hidden" aria-label="Link to this section: 5. Shipping, returns and delivery times are hidden">#</a></h3>
<p>Not knowing what shipping costs or when the order will arrive is one of the most common reasons people leave. Many stores only reveal this at checkout.</p>
<p><strong>Fix:</strong> state delivery times, shipping costs or free-shipping thresholds, and the returns policy on the product page, near the button.</p>
<h2 id="collection-and-navigation-problems">Collection and Navigation Problems<a class="prose-anchor" href="#collection-and-navigation-problems" aria-label="Link to this section: Collection and Navigation Problems">#</a></h2>
<h3 id="6-visitors-can-t-find-the-right-product">6. Visitors can&#39;t find the right product<a class="prose-anchor" href="#6-visitors-can-t-find-the-right-product" aria-label="Link to this section: 6. Visitors can't find the right product">#</a></h3>
<p>Collections with dozens of products and no useful filters push visitors to search, and many leave instead.</p>
<p><strong>Fix:</strong> filter by what customers actually choose on, such as size, concern, use or age, not just price and availability. Check your site search terms to see what people are looking for.</p>
<h3 id="7-the-ad-and-the-landing-page-don-t-match">7. The ad and the landing page don&#39;t match<a class="prose-anchor" href="#7-the-ad-and-the-landing-page-don-t-match" aria-label="Link to this section: 7. The ad and the landing page don't match">#</a></h3>
<p>An ad about one benefit that lands on a generic homepage or collection page breaks the visitor&#39;s train of thought.</p>
<p><strong>Fix:</strong> send each campaign to a page that continues the ad&#39;s message. For bigger campaigns, a dedicated <a href="/landing-page-developer/">landing page for paid traffic</a> usually outperforms a standard product page.</p>
<h2 id="speed-and-mobile-problems">Speed and Mobile Problems<a class="prose-anchor" href="#speed-and-mobile-problems" aria-label="Link to this section: Speed and Mobile Problems">#</a></h2>
<h3 id="8-pages-are-slow-on-mobile">8. Pages are slow on mobile<a class="prose-anchor" href="#8-pages-are-slow-on-mobile" aria-label="Link to this section: 8. Pages are slow on mobile">#</a></h3>
<p>Heavy images, unused app scripts and large sliders slow pages down, and on a phone connection every second of loading costs visitors.</p>
<p><strong>Fix:</strong> compress and correctly size images, remove apps you no longer use (and their leftover code), and avoid autoplay sliders on the first screen. Check key pages in Google&#39;s PageSpeed Insights on mobile.</p>
<h3 id="9-pop-ups-interrupt-before-they-help">9. Pop-ups interrupt before they help<a class="prose-anchor" href="#9-pop-ups-interrupt-before-they-help" aria-label="Link to this section: 9. Pop-ups interrupt before they help">#</a></h3>
<p>A discount pop-up that covers the page the moment someone arrives on mobile often costs more attention than it earns.</p>
<p><strong>Fix:</strong> delay pop-ups, show them on exit or after real engagement, and make them easy to close on a phone.</p>
<h2 id="cart-and-checkout-problems">Cart and Checkout Problems<a class="prose-anchor" href="#cart-and-checkout-problems" aria-label="Link to this section: Cart and Checkout Problems">#</a></h2>
<h3 id="10-surprise-costs-at-checkout">10. Surprise costs at checkout<a class="prose-anchor" href="#10-surprise-costs-at-checkout" aria-label="Link to this section: 10. Surprise costs at checkout">#</a></h3>
<p>Shipping, taxes or fees that appear for the first time at checkout are a classic reason for abandoned checkouts.</p>
<p><strong>Fix:</strong> show costs and free-shipping thresholds early, on the product page and in the cart. A progress bar towards free shipping in the cart can also lift order value.</p>
<h3 id="11-the-cart-creates-friction">11. The cart creates friction<a class="prose-anchor" href="#11-the-cart-creates-friction" aria-label="Link to this section: 11. The cart creates friction">#</a></h3>
<p>Cart pages that are slow, cluttered with upsells or hard to edit on mobile slow the path to checkout.</p>
<p><strong>Fix:</strong> keep the cart simple: clear items, easy quantity changes, one relevant upsell at most, and a prominent checkout button. Make sure express payment options such as Shop Pay are available.</p>
<h3 id="12-trust-signals-are-missing">12. Trust signals are missing<a class="prose-anchor" href="#12-trust-signals-are-missing" aria-label="Link to this section: 12. Trust signals are missing">#</a></h3>
<p>New visitors need reasons to trust a brand they met minutes ago, especially for higher-priced products.</p>
<p><strong>Fix:</strong> show guarantees, returns, secure payment options, real reviews and how to contact you, in the places where people decide: product pages and cart.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-decide-what-to-fix-first">How to Decide What to Fix First<a class="prose-anchor" href="#how-to-decide-what-to-fix-first" aria-label="Link to this section: How to Decide What to Fix First">#</a></h2>
<p>Not every problem is worth fixing first. Prioritise by:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Traffic:</strong> a fix on your top product page reaches far more visitors than one on a rarely visited page.</li>
<li><strong>Size of the drop:</strong> start at the funnel step where you lose the most people.</li>
<li><strong>Effort:</strong> quick fixes, such as moving reviews up or showing shipping costs, can go live in days.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="when-fixes-aren-t-enough">When Fixes Aren&#39;t Enough<a class="prose-anchor" href="#when-fixes-aren-t-enough" aria-label="Link to this section: When Fixes Aren't Enough">#</a></h2>
<p>If you find problems on almost every page, the issue may be the store&#39;s foundations rather than individual elements. At that point, a <a href="/shopify-store-redesign/">full Shopify store redesign</a> is often faster than fixing pages one by one. For help deciding, see <a href="/blog/when-to-redesign-shopify-store/">when a DTC brand should redesign its Shopify store</a>.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion<a class="prose-anchor" href="#conclusion" aria-label="Link to this section: Conclusion">#</a></h2>
<p>Most Shopify conversion problems come down to the same few things: visitors can&#39;t quickly understand the product, can&#39;t find the information they need to trust it, or hit friction on the way to checkout. Find the biggest drop in your funnel first, fix the high-traffic pages, and measure before and after each change.</p>
